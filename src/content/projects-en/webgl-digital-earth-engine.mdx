## Context

This is a WebGL engine for digital earth and digital twin scenarios. In the browser it needs to handle city-scale terrain, imagery, vector data, photogrammetry, BIM/glTF models, spatial analysis, underground pipelines and urban flood simulation.

The hard part is not simply drawing models. It is:

- integrating heterogeneous data sources under a unified coordinate system;
- keeping large scenes responsive through loading, scheduling and frame-rate stability;
- giving business teams reusable rendering capabilities rather than one-off effects;
- bringing analysis results back into the 3D scene.

## Technical work

The work falls into four areas:

1. **Rendering**: materials, lighting, post-processing, particles, dynamic line/area effects and business-specific render pipelines.
2. **Data integration**: extending 3D formats and sources, handling coordinate systems, hierarchy, transforms and style mapping.
3. **Performance and stability**: reducing unnecessary draw calls and state changes, improving loading and interaction for large datasets.
4. **Business support**: turning requirements from urban flood simulation, underground pipelines and digital twins into reusable engine capabilities.

## Key directions

### Specialized render pipelines

Business scenarios often need effects that the engine does not provide out of the box, such as:

- water surfaces, heatmaps and rainfall/river coupling for urban flood simulation;
- clipping, transparency, occlusion and flow animation for underground pipelines;
- analytical representations for building blocks, administrative areas and skylines.

The approach is to split each effect into three layers — data preparation, render pipeline, interaction/analysis — and reuse the existing render flow instead of writing a separate system for every project.

### Geometry and spatial analysis

GIS analysis is more than visualization. Geometry editing, boolean operations, rectangle picking, skyline analysis and cross-sections require geometric correctness, screen interaction and rendering performance at the same time.

This is where geometry algorithms and 3D rendering meet in practice.

## Outcomes

- Built a reusable set of 3D rendering and spatial analysis capabilities for multiple scenarios.
- Turned several project-specific effects into engine capabilities, reducing duplicate development.
- Validated loading, rendering and interaction stability with real city-scale datasets.

## Reflection

Engine work can easily become feature accumulation. The difficult part is drawing boundaries: what goes into the engine, what stays in the business layer, what should be a generic pipeline and what must be customized. That judgment is more valuable than any single flashy effect.
