## Context

Desktop digital earth software serves transportation planning, engineering construction and related industries. Users need to load terrain, imagery, vector data and models onto a 3D globe, then view, measure, analyze and export results.

## Technical work

The work mainly involved:

- data loading and display in 3D scenes;
- overlaying terrain, imagery, vector data and models;
- basic measurement and analysis tools;
- feature customization and issue diagnosis for industry projects.

## Technical environment

Desktop C++ engines and browser engines have different priorities:

- desktop engines care more about large datasets and long-running stability;
- the render pipeline is closer to the metal and debugging is more expensive;
- industry users have stricter requirements for coordinates, precision and formats.

This combination of **real GIS data and 3D rendering** is quite different from pure graphics effects.

## Outcomes

- Contributed to multiple feature modules of the digital earth engine.
- Supported real projects in transportation planning and engineering construction.
- Built a foundation in 3D GIS data loading, rendering and interaction.

## Reflection

The most important takeaway is not a specific feature, but understanding the relationship between engine capabilities, industry data and project delivery. That relationship shapes many decisions in 3D engine and data-production work.
